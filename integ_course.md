# Course: Integral Calculus and Differential Equations, Accumulating Change

**Slug:** `integral-calculus` (a course alongside `calculus`, which covers functions, limits and differential calculus; same block schema, same seed-script pattern). Prefix `integ`: files `src/modules/content/integ-chapter-N-content.ts`, exports `integChapterNLessons`, question ids `integN-M-qK`.

**Purpose:** Build the intuition that integration is *accumulation*: adding up thin strips of something that changes. Antiderivatives come first, as differentiation run backwards; the definite integral comes next, as the limit of a sum of strips; the Fundamental Theorem then ties the two together. From there the course builds the JEE toolkit (substitution, parts, partial fractions, the properties of definite integrals, areas) and ends with differential equations, where the unknown is a whole function and integration is how we find it.

**End state:** Given any integral or differential equation in a JEE paper, the student can reconstruct the method from:

```text
derivative table, read backwards        → standard integrals
chain rule backwards   → substitution      product rule backwards → parts
algebra first          → partial fractions, completing the square, trig identities
sum of thin strips     → definite integral (area, limits of sums)
F(x) = ∫ₐˣ f  has  F′ = f                → evaluation and Leibniz rule
symmetry of [a, b]     → King's rule, even/odd, periodicity
dy/dx = F(x, y)        → separate, substitute, or multiply by an integrating factor
```

They should never need a memorised list of "standard results" they cannot rederive: every formula in the course is derived from a derivative, a picture, or a substitution, and each mastery lesson hides the formula sheet.

**Three threads run through every chapter**
1. **Intuition + visualization:** strips, areas and slope fields move on screen (Riemann sums converging, the area-so-far growing at rate f, horizontal vs vertical strips, solution curves threading a slope field) before the algebra arrives.
2. **Derivation drill:** every integral is checked by differentiating; every standard form is derived once by substitution; every property of definite integrals is proved with a one-line substitution.
3. **JEE pay-off:** each lesson ends with JEE Main/Advanced-style questions (single-correct, multiple-correct, numerical value) and each chapter with a "reconstruct it" mastery lesson.

**Interactives (new):**
- `integ-riemann-sum`: left/right/midpoint/trapezoid sums with an n slider converging to the exact integral; `accumulate` mode sweeps the upper limit to show $A(x) = \int_a^x f$ growing at rate $f(x)$ (the Fundamental Theorem).
- `integ-area-between`: region between two curves shaded, with vertical strips ($y = f(x)$, $y = g(x)$) or horizontal strips ($x = f(y)$, $x = g(y)$); crossings found, signed integral vs area compared.
- `integ-slope-field`: slope field of $\frac{dy}{dx} = F(x, y)$ with a draggable start point and RK4 solution curves; with $F$ depending on $x$ alone it shows the "+ C" family of antiderivatives.

Reused: `graph-explorer`, `transform-playground`, `calc-tangent-explorer`, `function-plot`-based components where they fit, with captions.

## Chapter 0: Antiderivatives

**Purpose:** read the derivative table backwards; understand why the answer is a family of curves and what pins down one member; rewrite integrands with algebra and trig identities until every piece is in the table.

| Lesson | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 0.1 | Undoing the Derivative (`undoing-the-derivative`) | If $F' = f$ then $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$, and so is $F + C$ | Hook: a speedometer record, recover the distance travelled. Definition; why the $+C$ (derivative of a constant is 0; MVT: two antiderivatives on an interval differ by a constant); notation $\int f(x)\,dx$; checking by differentiating. Family of curves as vertical shifts. Interactive: `integ-slope-field` with $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x$ (parabolas). Misconception killed: "the antiderivative is unique" |
| 0.2 | The Standard Integrals Table (`standard-integrals`) | Every row of the derivative table gives a row of the integral table | Power rule $\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C$ ($n \ne -1$), $\int \frac{dx}{x} = \ln\lvert x\rvert + C$ (why the modulus: check $x < 0$), $e^x$, $a^x$, $\sin$, $\cos$, $\sec^2$, $\csc^2$, $\sec\tan$, $\csc\cot$, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$, $\frac{1}{1+x^2}$, $\frac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}$; $\int f(ax+b)\,dx = \frac1a F(ax+b)$. ## Chapter 1: Integration by Substitution

**Purpose:** the chain rule backwards; a catalogue of patterns ($f'/f$, $f' f^n$), trig powers, trig substitutions, and the standard forms derived once and used everywhere.

| Lesson | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 1.1 | Substitution: The Chain Rule in Reverse (`u-substitution`) | $\int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx = \int f(u)\,du$ | Derivation from the chain rule; spotting the inner function and its derivative; $du = g'(x)dx$ bookkeeping; $\int\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}dx = \ln\lvert f\rvert$, $\int f'(x)[f(x)]^n dx = \frac{f^{n+1}}{n+1}$; $\int\tan x$, $\cot x$, $\sec x$, $\csc x$ derived; $\int\frac{e^x}{1+e^x}$, $\int\frac{\ln x}{x}$, $\int\frac{\sin(\ln x)}{x}$, $\int\frac{1}{x\ln x}$; back-substituting $x$ in terms of $u$ ($\int x\sqrt{x+1}$). Misconception killed: "change $x$ to $u$ but leave $dx$" |
| 1.2 | Integrals of Trigonometric Powers (`trig-powers`) | Odd power: peel one factor off for $du$; even powers: halve the angle | $\int\sin^m x\cos^n x$: odd $m$ or $n$ (substitute the other), both even (double-angle); $\int\tan^n x$ via $\tan^2 = \sec^2 - 1$; $\int\sec^n x\tan^m x$ (even power of sec: $u = \tan x$; odd power of tan: $u = \sec x$); $\int\frac{\sin^3 x}{\cos^5 x}$; $\int\frac{dx}{\sin x\cos^3 x}$; $\int\sin^4 x$. Misconception killed: "every power needs a reduction formula" |
| 1.3 | Trigonometric Substitutions (`trig-substitution`) | A square root of a sum or difference of squares is a side of a right triangle | $\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$: $x = a\sin\theta$; $\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}$: $x = a\tan\theta$; $\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}$: $x = a\sec\theta$; triangle to convert back; $\sqrt{\frac{a - x}{a + x}}$ with $x = a\cos 2\theta$; $\frac{1}{(1+x^2)^{3/2}}$; $\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$. Misconception killed: "forget to convert back from $\theta$ to $x$" |
| 1.4 | The Standard Forms (`standard-forms`) | Six forms, each derived once by substitution | $\int\frac{dx}{x^2+a^2} = \frac1a\tan^{-1}\frac xa$; $\int\frac{dx}{x^2-a^2} = \frac{1}{2a}\ln\left\lvert\frac{x-a}{x+a}\right\rvert$; $\int\frac{dx}{a^2-x^2}$; $\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}} = \sin^{-1}\frac xa$; $\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2\pm a^2}} = \ln\lvert x + \sqrt{x^2 \pm a^2}\rvert$; $\int\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\,dx$, $\int\sqrt{x^2\pm a^2}\,dx$ (area picture for the first). Table of all forms. Misconception killed: "$\int\frac{dx}{x^2+4} = \tan^{-1}\frac x2$" (missing $\frac12$) |
| 1.5 | Quadratics: Completing the Square (`quadratic-integrands`) | Every quadratic is $a[(x + p)^2 \pm q^2]$, which lands on a standard form | $\int\frac{dx}{ax^2+bx+c}$, $\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{ax^2+bx+c}}$, $\int\sqrt{ax^2+bx+c}\,dx$; linear over quadratic: $px + q = A\frac{d}{dx}(ax^2+bx+c) + B$; $\int\frac{px+q}{\sqrt{ax^2+bx+c}}$; sign of the discriminant decides tan⁻¹ vs log. Misconception killed: "the leading coefficient can be ignored" |
| 1.6 | Rational Functions of sin and cos (`trig-rational-functions`) | $t = \tan\frac x2$ turns any rational function of $\sin x$, $\cos x$ into a rational function of $t$ | $\sin x = \frac{2t}{1+t^2}$, $\cos x = \frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}$, $dx = \frac{2\,dt}{1+t^2}$; $\int\frac{dx}{a + b\cos x}$ (three cases $a > b$, $a < b$, $a = b$), $\int\frac{dx}{a + b\sin x}$, $\int\frac{dx}{a\sin x + b\cos x}$ via $R\sin(x + \alpha)$; $\int\frac{dx}{a\sin^2x + b\cos^2x}$ (divide by $\cos^2$); $\int\frac{a\sin x + b\cos x}{c\sin x + d\cos x}$ as $A\cdot\text{den} + B\cdot\text{den}'$. Misconception killed: "$t = \tan\frac x2$ is always the best route" |
| 1.7 | Chapter 1 Mastery (`chapter-1-mastery`) | Choose the substitution | Mixed mastery: pattern recognition, trig powers, trig substitution, standard forms with completed squares, $\frac{px+q}{\text{quadratic}}$, $t = \tan\frac x2$, JEE-style "if $\int\ldots = A\ln\ldots + B\tan^{-1}\ldots$, find $A + B$" |

## Chapter 2: Integration by Parts and Partial Fractions

**Purpose:** the product rule backwards (with ILATE to choose $u$), repeated and cyclic parts, the $e^x(f + f')$ pattern; partial fractions for every factor type and the classic substitution tricks; a first look at reduction formulae.

| Lesson | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 2.1 | Integration by Parts (`integration-by-parts`) | $\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$, from $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$ | Derivation; ILATE (inverse trig, log, algebraic, trig, exponential) as a heuristic for $u$ and why it works (differentiating $u$ should simplify it); $\int xe^x$, $\int x\sin x$, $\int\ln x$, $\int\tan^{-1}x$, $\int x\ln x$, $\int\sin^{-1}x$, $\int x\sec^2 x$; a bad choice of $u$ shown failing. Misconception killed: "$\int fg = f\int g$" |
| 2.2 | Repeated and Cyclic Parts (`repeated-and-cyclic-parts`) | Apply parts again until the integral vanishes or returns | $\int x^2e^x$, $\int x^3e^x$ with the tabular (DI) method; cyclic: $\int e^{ax}\sin bx = \frac{e^{ax}}{a^2+b^2}(a\sin bx - b\cos bx)$, $\int e^{ax}\cos bx$; $\int\sec^3 x$; $\int\sin(\ln x)$. Misconception killed: "a returning integral means you went in a circle and failed" |
| 2.3 | The e^x(f + f′) Pattern (`ex-f-plus-f-prime`) | $\int e^x[f(x) + f'(x)]\,dx = e^xf(x) + C$ | Proof by differentiating (and by parts); spotting $f$: $e^x\left(\frac1x - \frac1{x^2}\right)$, $e^x\frac{x}{(1+x)^2}$, $e^x\frac{1 + \sin x}{1 + \cos x}$, $e^x(\tan x + \sec^2 x)$, $e^x\frac{x^2+1}{(x+1)^2}$; companions $\int[f(x) + xf'(x)]dx = xf(x)$ and $\int e^{kx}[kf + f']$. Misconception killed: "every $e^x$ times something needs parts twice" |
| 2.4 | Partial Fractions: Linear Factors (`partial-fractions-linear`) | Undo "adding fractions": split a proper rational function into simple pieces | Proper vs improper (divide first); distinct linear factors with the cover-up rule; repeated linear factors ($\frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{(x-a)^2}$); $\int\frac{dx}{(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}$, $\int\frac{x^2}{(x-1)(x+2)}$, $\int\frac{dx}{x(x+1)^2}$. Misconception killed: "$\frac{1}{(x-1)^2(x+2)} = \frac{A}{(x-1)^2} + \frac{B}{x+2}$" (missing term) |
| 2.5 | Partial Fractions: Quadratic Factors and Tricks (`partial-fractions-quadratic`) | An irreducible quadratic gets a linear numerator; substitutions shrink the degree | $\frac{Ax + B}{x^2 + px + q}$ terms; $\int\frac{dx}{(x+1)(x^2+1)}$, $\int\frac{dx}{x^3+1}$; substitution $t = x^2$ for $\frac{x}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}$; $\int\frac{dx}{x(x^n+1)}$; dividing by $x^2$: $\int\frac{x^2+1}{x^4+x^2+1}$, $\int\frac{x^2-1}{x^4+1}$, $\int\frac{x^2+1}{x^4+1}$ with $t = x \mp \frac1x$; $\int\sqrt{\tan x}$ (JEE classic). Misconception killed: "a quadratic factor gets a constant numerator" |
| 2.6 | Reduction Formulae (`reduction-formulae`) | Relate $I_n$ to $I_{n-1}$ or $I_{n-2}$ by parts | $I_n = \int\sin^n x\,dx$; $\int\tan^n x$: $I_n + I_{n-2} = \frac{\tan^{n-1}x}{n-1}$; $\int x^ne^x$: $I_n = x^ne^x - nI_{n-1}$; $\int(\ln x)^n$; $\int\sec^n x$; JEE-style "if $I_n + I_{n-2} = \ldots$, find" questions. Misconception killed: "a reduction formula gives the answer directly" (it gives a ladder down to $I_0$ or $I_1$) |
| 2.7 | Chapter 2 Mastery (`chapter-2-mastery`) | Parts or partial fractions? | Mixed mastery: ILATE choices, cyclic integrals, $e^x(f + f')$ recognition, partial fractions of every type, $\frac{x^2\pm1}{x^4+kx^2+1}$ tricks, reduction ladders, JEE-style numerics |

## Chapter 3: The Definite Integral

**Purpose:** area as a limit of sums of thin strips; evaluate limits of sums by recognising them as integrals; the Fundamental Theorem in both parts; evaluate with substitution and parts; differentiate integrals with variable limits.

| Lesson | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 3.1 | The Area Problem and Riemann Sums (`riemann-sums`) | Approximate the area by rectangles; thinner rectangles, better estimate | The area under $y = x^2$ on $[0, 1]$ by 4, 10, 100 strips; left, right, midpoint, trapezoid; over- vs under-estimates for increasing functions; sigma notation and $\sum k$, $\sum k^2$, $\sum k^3$. Interactive: `integ-riemann-sum` on $x^2$ over $[0, 1]$. Misconception killed: "more rectangles always means a larger estimate" |
| 3.2 | The Definite Integral as a Limit of a Sum (`definite-integral-as-limit`) | $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum f(x_i)\,\Delta x$ | Definition with $\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{n}$, $x_i = a + i\Delta x$; evaluate $\int_0^2 x^2$, $\int_1^3(2x+1)$, $\int_0^1 e^x$ (geometric series) from the definition; signed area (below the axis counts negative); $\int_a^a = 0$, $\int_b^a = -\int_a^b$. Interactive: `integ-riemann-sum` on a function dipping below the axis. Misconception killed: "a definite integral is always an area" (it is a signed area) |
| 3.3 | Limits of Sums as Integrals (`limits-of-sums`) | $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^{n} f\!\left(\frac rn\right) = \int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$ | Recipe: pull out $\frac1n$, write the summand as $f(r/n)$, read off limits from $\frac rn$ at the first and last $r$ (e.g. $\sum_{r=1}^{2n}$ gives $\int_0^2$); $\sum\frac{n}{n^2+r^2}$, $\sum\frac{1}{n+r}$, $\sum\frac{r^2}{n^3}$, $\sum\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^2 - r^2}}$; products via logs ($\lim\left(\frac{n!}{n^n}\right)^{1/n} = \frac1e$, $\lim\left[\prod(1 + \frac rn)\right]^{1/n} = \frac4e$). Misconception killed: "the upper limit is always 1" |
| 3.4 | The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (`fundamental-theorem`) | The area-so-far function grows at rate $f$: $\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)$; hence $\int_a^b f = F(b) - F(a)$ | Accumulation function $A(x)$; part 1 from the thin-strip picture and a squeeze; part 2 from part 1 (two antiderivatives differ by a constant); evaluation notation $[F(x)]_a^b$; constant of integration cancels; continuity needed (the $\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dx}{x^2}$ trap). Interactive: `integ-riemann-sum` in accumulate mode. Misconception killed: "$\int_{-1}^1\frac{dx}{x^2} = -2$" |
| 3.5 | Evaluating Definite Integrals (`evaluating-definite-integrals`) | Substitute and change the limits; or integrate by parts with limits | Substitution with new limits (no back-substitution); parts: $[uv]_a^b - \int v\,du$; $\int_0^1\frac{x}{1+x^2}$, $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3 x$, $\int_0^1 x e^x$, $\int_1^e\ln x$, $\int_0^1\tan^{-1}x$, $\int_0^{\pi/4}\frac{dx}{1+\cos 2x}$; invalid substitutions (non-monotonic or undefined across the interval, e.g. $t = \tan x$ over $[0, \pi]$). Misconception killed: "keep the old limits after substituting" |
| 3.6 | Differentiating Integrals: the Leibniz Rule (`leibniz-rule`) | $\frac{d}{dx}\int_{g(x)}^{h(x)}f(t)\,dt = f(h(x))h'(x) - f(g(x))g'(x)$ | Derived from FTC + chain rule; $\frac{d}{dx}\int_{x^2}^{x^3}\frac{dt}{\ln t}$; limits $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\int_0^{x^2}\sin\sqrt t\,dt}{x^3}$ with L'Hôpital; integral equations $f(x) = x + \int_0^x f(t)\,dt$; extrema of $F(x) = \int_0^x (t-1)(t-2)\,dt$; Advanced: differentiating under the integral sign (brief). Misconception killed: "forget the chain-rule factor $h'(x)$" |
| 3.7 | Chapter 3 Mastery (`chapter-3-mastery`) | Sums, areas and antiderivatives, linked | Mixed mastery: Riemann estimates, integral from the definition, limits of sums (including products), FTC evaluation, substitution with limits, Leibniz rule with limits and integral equations |

## Chapter 4: Properties of Definite Integrals

**Purpose:** the JEE workhorse chapter. Every property is a one-line substitution; most hard definite integrals fall to King's rule, symmetry, periodicity or splitting at breakpoints.

| Lesson | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 4.1 | Basic Properties and King's Rule (`kings-rule`) | Reflecting the interval, $x \to a + b - x$, leaves the integral unchanged | Dummy variable, reversing limits, additivity over $[a, c] \cup [c, b]$; $\int_a^b f(x)dx = \int_a^b f(a+b-x)dx$ (proof by substitution, picture as reflection); $\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^n x}{\sin^n x + \cos^n x} = \frac\pi4$; $\int_0^\pi\frac{x\sin x}{1+\cos^2x} = \frac{\pi^2}{4}$; $\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\sin x = -\frac\pi2\ln2$; $\int_0^{\pi/4}\ln(1+\tan x) = \frac\pi8\ln2$; "add the two forms" technique. Misconception killed: "King's rule only works for trig functions" |
| 4.2 | Even, Odd and the 2a Rules (`symmetric-intervals`) | Symmetry halves the work or kills the integral | $\int_{-a}^a f = 2\int_0^a f$ (even) or $0$ (odd), proof; split $f$ into even + odd parts; $\int_0^{2a}f = 2\int_0^a f$ if $f(2a-x) = f(x)$, $0$ if $f(2a - x) = -f(x)$; $\int_{-a}^{a}\frac{f(x)}{1+e^{x}}$-type tricks; $\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos x}{1+e^x}$; $\int_0^\pi\frac{dx}{1 + \sin x}$ type with $2a$. Interactive: `integ-area-between` on an odd function over $[-a, a]$ (signed integral 0, area not). Misconception killed: "an odd integrand over any interval gives 0" |
| 4.3 | Periodic Functions (`periodic-functions`) | Every period contributes the same amount | $\int_a^{a+T}f$ is independent of $a$ (proof); $\int_0^{nT}f = n\int_0^T f$; $\int_0^{n\pi}\lvert\sin x\rvert = 2n$; $\int_0^{100\pi}\sqrt{1 - \cos 2x}$; $\int_0^{n}\{x\}\,dx = \frac n2$; $\int_a^{b}$ with non-integer number of periods (split off whole periods); periods of $\lvert\sin x\rvert + \lvert\cos x\rvert$. Misconception killed: "the period of $\lvert\sin x\rvert$ is $2\pi$" |
| 4.4 | Modulus, Greatest Integer and Piecewise Integrands (`piecewise-integrands`) | Split at every breakpoint, then integrate each piece | $\int\lvert f\rvert$: split at the zeros of $f$; $\int_0^2[x^2]$, $\int_0^n[x]$, $\int_{-1}^{2}[x]$, $\int_0^2 x[x]$; $\{x\}$; $\operatorname{sgn}$; $\max/\min$ of two functions; $\int_{-1}^{1}\lvert x\rvert x$-type parity shortcuts; $\int_0^{\pi}[\,2\sin x\,]$-type. Interactive: `integ-area-between` with $\lvert x^2 - 1\rvert$-type splitting. Misconception killed: "$\int\lvert f\rvert = \lvert\int f\rvert$" |
| 4.5 | Bounds and Inequalities (`bounds-and-inequalities`) | If $m \le f \le M$ on $[a, b]$, then $m(b - a) \le \int_a^b f \le M(b - a)$ | Comparison ($f \le g \Rightarrow \int f \le \int g$); $\lvert\int f\rvert \le \int\lvert f\rvert$; bounds for $\int_0^1 e^{x^2}$, $\int_0^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4 - x^2 - x^3}}$ (between $\frac\pi6$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}$-type), $\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin x}{x}$; mean value theorem for integrals, average value $\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f$; comparing $\int_0^1 x^n$ vs $x^{n+1}$, $\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}$ vs $e^{-x}$; JEE "which is larger" questions. Misconception killed: "a bigger integrand somewhere means a bigger integral" |
| 4.6 | Wallis Formula and Definite Reduction (`wallis-formula`) | $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^n x = \frac{n-1}{n}\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^{n-2}x$, so the ladder ends at $1$ or $\frac\pi2$ | Derivation by parts (boundary term vanishes); Wallis product $\frac{(n-1)(n-3)\cdots}{n(n-2)\cdots}\cdot(\frac\pi2 \text{ or } 1)$; $\cos^n$ by King's rule; $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^m x\cos^n x$ (formula and examples); $\int_0^{\pi}\sin^n$, $\int_0^{2\pi}$ via symmetry; $\int_0^1 x^m(1-x)^n$ for small integers; $\int_0^\infty x^ne^{-x} = n!$ (Advanced). Misconception killed: "Wallis for odd $n$ ends with $\frac\pi2$" |
| 4.7 | Chapter 4 Mastery (`chapter-4-mastery`) | Spot the symmetry first | Mixed mastery: King's rule, $\frac{1}{1+e^x}$ trick, periodicity with modulus, greatest-integer integrals, bounds, Wallis, JEE Advanced style multiple-correct |

| 5.1 | Area Under a Curve (`area-under-a-curve`) | Area is $\int\lvert f\rvert$, not $\int f$ | Area bounded by $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis and $x = a$, $x = b$; parts below the axis; area with the $y$-axis using $x = g(y)$; $y = x^2 - 4x + 3$ with the axis; $y = \sin x$ over $[0, 2\pi]$; $y = \ln x$ region (both strip directions). | 5.2 | Area Between Two Curves (`area-between-curves`) | Area $= \int_a^b(\text{top} - \text{bottom})\,dx$ between the intersections | Strip picture; find intersections first; curves that cross (split); horizontal strips $\int(\text{right} - \text{left})\,dy$ when the left/right boundaries are single curves: $y^2 = 2x$ and $y = x - 4$ (area 18); $y = x^2$ and $y = x$; $y = x^3$ and $y = x$. Interactive: `integ-area-between` in both modes. Misconception killed: "subtract in any order and take the modulus at the end" |
| 5.3 | Areas with Parabolas, Circles and Ellipses (`conic-areas`) | Symmetry plus the standard forms | Ellipse $\pi ab$ by integration; circle segments; $y^2 = 4ax$ and $x^2 = 4ay$ enclose $\frac{16a^2}{3}$; parabola and its latus rectum ($\frac{8a^2}{3}$); circle $x^2 + y^2 = 16$ and parabola $y^2 = 6x$; region between circle and ellipse; area under a parabolic arch (Archimedes: $\frac23$ of the rectangle). Misconception killed: "integrate the full circle equation $y = \sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$ and forget the lower half" |
| 5.4 | Modulus Curves and Regions Defined by Inequalities (`modulus-and-inequality-regions`) | Sketch the region first; let symmetry and modulus split it | $\lvert x\rvert + \lvert y\rvert \le a$; $y \ge \lvert x - 1\rvert$ with a parabola; $\{(x, y): x^2 \le y \le \lvert x\rvert\}$; $y \le e^{-\lvert x\rvert}$ type; region $\{y^2 \le 4x,\ x^2 + y^2 \le 5\}$-type; area between $f$ and its inverse (reflect in $y = x$); regions with $[x]$; JEE inequality-region problems. Misconception killed: "every region needs vertical strips" |
| 5.5 | Area Problems with Parameters (`area-with-parameters`) | Area as a function of a parameter: solve, compare or optimise | Find $a$ so that $x = a$ bisects the area under $y = \sqrt x$; line $y = mx$ cutting the region under a parabola in half; area between $y = x^2$ and the line through $(1, 2)$ minimised; area bounded by a curve and its tangent; ratio of areas into which a parabola divides a circle; area as a limit (area under $y = x^n$ vs $n$). Misconception killed: "the area answer must be an integer or nice fraction" |

## Chapter 6: Differential Equations

**Purpose:** equations whose unknown is a function. Classify (order, degree), form them by eliminating constants, then solve the four JEE types (separable, homogeneous, linear, Bernoulli/reducible) and apply them to growth, cooling, mixing and geometry.

| Lesson | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 6.1 | What a Differential Equation Is (`what-is-a-differential-equation`) | A rule for the slope at every point; a solution is a curve that obeys it everywhere | Slope-field picture; order (highest derivative) and degree (power of the highest derivative once the equation is a polynomial in derivatives; undefined for $\sin y'$); verifying a solution by substitution; general (with $n$ arbitrary constants) vs particular solution; existence of a solution through a point. Interactive: `integ-slope-field` for $\frac{dy}{dx} = x - y$. Misconception killed: "degree of $y'' = \sqrt{1 + y'}$ is $\frac12$" (square first: degree 2) |
| 6.2 | Forming Differential Equations (`forming-differential-equations`) | $n$ arbitrary constants need $n$ differentiations to eliminate | Lines through the origin, $y = mx + c$, circles through the origin with centre on an axis, parabolas $y^2 = 4ax$, $y = Ae^{x} + Be^{-x}$ ($y'' = y$), $y = A\cos x + B\sin x$, family of circles touching the axes; count essential constants ($y = Ae^{x+B}$ has one). Misconception killed: "the order equals the number of constants written" |
| 6.3 | Variables Separable (`variables-separable`) | $\frac{dy}{dx} = g(x)h(y)$: put every $y$ with $dy$ and every $x$ with $dx$, then integrate | Method; $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y}$, $\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{x-y}$, $\frac{dy}{dx} = (1 + y^2)x$; particular solutions; reducible: $\frac{dy}{dx} = f(ax + by + c)$ via $v = ax + by + c$ ($\frac{dy}{dx} = (x + y)^2$); lost solutions when dividing by $h(y)$. Interactive: `integ-slope-field` on $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac xy$ (circles). Misconception killed: "$\int\frac{dy}{y} = \frac{y^0}{0}$" / forgetting the constant until the end |
| 6.4 | Homogeneous Equations (`homogeneous-equations`) | If $\frac{dy}{dx} = F\left(\frac yx\right)$, put $y = vx$ | Homogeneous functions of degree $n$; test $f(\lambda x, \lambda y) = \lambda^n f(x, y)$; $y = vx$, $\frac{dy}{dx} = v + x\frac{dv}{dx}$ separates; $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x + y}{x}$, $(x^2 + y^2)dx = 2xy\,dy$, $x\frac{dy}{dx} = y + \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$; reducible $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{a_1x + b_1y + c_1}{a_2x + b_2y + c_2}$ by shifting the origin (Advanced) or $v = a_1x + b_1y$ when the lines are parallel. Misconception killed: "homogeneous means the right side is 0" |
| 6.5 | Linear First-Order Equations (`linear-equations`) | Multiply by $e^{\int P\,dx}$ and the left side becomes one derivative | $\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x)$; integrating factor derived from the product rule; $y\cdot\text{IF} = \int Q\cdot\text{IF}\,dx + C$; $\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac yx = x^2$, $\frac{dy}{dx} + y\tan x = \sec x$, $\frac{dy}{dx} - y = e^{x}$ (resonance-free example), linear in $x$: $\frac{dx}{dy} + P(y)x = Q(y)$ (e.g. $(x + y^2)\frac{dy}{dx} = y$). Misconception killed: "IF $= e^{\int P}$ needs a $+C$" |
| 6.6 | Bernoulli and Other Reducible Equations (`bernoulli-and-exact-forms`) | A substitution turns the equation linear or exact | Bernoulli $\frac{dy}{dx} + Py = Qy^n$: divide by $y^n$, $z = y^{1-n}$; equations like $\sec^2 y\frac{dy}{dx} + P\tan y = Q$ ($z = \tan y$); exact differentials by inspection: $d(xy)$, $d\left(\frac yx\right)$, $d\left(\frac xy\right)$, $d\ln\left(\frac yx\right)$, $d\tan^{-1}\frac yx$, $d\left(\frac{x^2+y^2}{2}\right)$; $x\,dy - y\,dx = x^2\,dx$-type (Advanced). Misconception killed: "Bernoulli needs $z = y^n$" |
| 6.7 | Applications: Growth, Cooling, Mixing and Curves (`applications-of-differential-equations`) | Translate the story into "rate = …", solve, then use the data to fix constants | Exponential growth/decay ($\frac{dN}{dt} = kN$), half-life; Newton's law of cooling; mixing tank (linear DE); curves from geometric conditions (subtangent, subnormal, tangent intercepts, slope at each point); orthogonal trajectories (replace $y'$ by $-\frac{1}{y'}$) intro. Misconception killed: "plug in the data before solving" |
| 6.8 | Chapter 6 Mastery (`chapter-6-mastery`) | Classify, then solve | Mixed mastery: order/degree, formation, every solution method, applications, JEE Main numerical answers and an Advanced multiple-correct |
