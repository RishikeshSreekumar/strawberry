# Course: Oscillations, Waves and Thermal Physics

**Slug:** `oscillations-waves-thermal` (course 3 of the JEE physics sequence, after `motion-forces-energy` and `momentum-rotation-gravitation`; same block schema, same seed-script pattern.) File/quiz prefix `owt`; content files `src/modules/content/owt-chapter-{0..5}-content.ts` exporting `owtChapter{N}Lessons`; quiz ids `owt{chapter}-{lesson}-q{n}`.

**Purpose:** Show that one small idea, *a restoring force proportional to displacement*, produces the sine curves of a spring, a pendulum, a guitar string, an organ pipe and a passing ambulance siren; then carry the same "many particles, simple rules" thinking into matter in bulk (elasticity, fluids, surface tension) and into heat (temperature as molecular motion, the first and second laws). Depth: CBSE Class 11 plus JEE Main and Advanced.

**End state:** Given any oscillation, wave or thermal question, the student can reconstruct the answer from:

```text
F = −kx  →  a = −ω²x  →  x = A cos(ωt + φ)  (the shadow of a turning arrow)
wave = SHM passed along:  y = A sin(kx − ωt),  v = ω/k = fλ,  v = √(T/μ) or √(γp/ρ)
boundaries pick the wavelengths  →  standing waves, pipes;  moving sources squash them  →  Doppler
bulk matter:  stress/strain, p = p₀ + ρgh, ½ρv² + p + ρgh = const, 2T/r
heat:  pV = nRT = ⅓Nm·v²_rms,  U = (f/2)nRT,  ΔU = Q − W,  W = area under p–V,  η ≤ 1 − T_C/T_H
```

They should never need a memorised list of pipe formulas, Doppler cases or process tables. They should be able to say *why* a closed pipe has only odd harmonics (a node is forced at the closed end), *why* an adiabat is steeper than an isotherm (the gas cools as it expands, so pressure falls faster, by exactly the factor γ), and *why* $v_{rms}$ doubles only when the temperature quadruples.

**Three threads run through every chapter**
1. **Intuition + visualization:** every new object appears first as something that moves (a block on a spring with its shadow circle, a string you can step through harmonics, crests bunching in front of a moving source, a gas whose molecules speed up as you heat it, a p–V loop whose area is the work) before it appears as a formula.
2. **Derivation from a small core:** results come from Newton's second law, conservation of energy and mass, the wave picture and the ideal-gas / first-law pair; nothing is stated cold. Each chapter's mastery lesson hides the formula sheet.
3. **JEE pay-off:** every lesson has at least one JEE-Main-style numerical worked in numbered steps (with *Why this step:* notes); every mastery lesson includes JEE-Advanced-flavoured multi-step problems. SI units throughout; $g = 10$ m/s² unless stated (say so in the first example of each lesson); $R = 8.314$ J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ (or $25/3$ where a problem wants clean numbers, flagged).

## Chapter 0: Simple Harmonic Motion

**Slug:** `simple-harmonic-motion`

**Purpose:** establish the SHM condition $a = -\omega^2 x$ as the one test for "is this SHM?", build $x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$ as the shadow of uniform circular motion, and apply it to springs, pendulums and other restoring forces, ending with damping and resonance.

Periodic vs oscillatory vs SHM (definition callouts). Restoring force from a potential-energy minimum ($U \approx \tfrac12 kx^2$ near any minimum, link to mfe PE curves). SHM condition $a = -\omega^2 x$ with $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$ derived from $F = ma$. Interactive: `owt-wave-lab` (mode `shm`, spring, `graphs: ["x","a"]`, `sliders: ["amplitude","omega"]`), watch $a$ always point back to $x = 0$ and be largest at the ends. | 0.2 | `the-shm-equation` | Shadow of a Turning Arrow | $x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$ is the projection of uniform circular motion; amplitude, phase, period | Hook: a peg on a turntable lit from the side, its shadow moves exactly like a block on a spring. ## Chapter 1: Waves on a String

**Slug:** `waves-on-a-string`

**Purpose:** a wave is a disturbance that travels while each particle only oscillates in place. Build the travelling-wave equation, derive the speed on a string, then superpose waves to get interference, reflection and standing waves.

| Lesson | Slug | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ---- | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 1.1 | `what-a-wave-carries` | What a Wave Carries | Energy and shape travel; matter stays | Hook: a stadium wave, the crowd stays in their seats. Pulses on a rope, transverse vs longitudinal (slinky), mechanical vs EM. A pulse $y = f(x - vt)$: the same shape shifted, derived from "the shape at time $t$ is the shape at 0 moved by $vt$". Interactive: `owt-wave-lab` (mode `traveling`, `showGhost: true`), point P only moves up and down. Worked examples: is $y = \frac{2}{(x - 3t)^2 + 1}$ a travelling wave, which way and how fast? | 1.2 | `the-wave-equation` | The Sinusoidal Travelling Wave | $y = A\sin(kx - \omega t + \phi)$, $k = 2\pi/\lambda$, $\omega = 2\pi f$, $v = \omega/k = f\lambda$ | Build it by feeding SHM of the end into the string. ## Chapter 2: Sound Waves

**Slug:** `sound-waves`

**Purpose:** carry the string toolkit to sound: a longitudinal wave that is equally a displacement wave and a pressure wave. Derive its speed, measure its loudness, trap it in pipes, beat it against itself and shift it with motion.

| Lesson | Slug | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ---- | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 2.1 | `sound-as-a-pressure-wave` | Sound Is Squeezed Air | Layers of air oscillate along the travel direction; compressions and rarefactions are pressure crests and troughs | Hook: a candle flame flickering in front of a speaker. Longitudinal displacement $s = s_0\sin(kx - \omega t)$ gives pressure $\Delta p = -B\,\partial s/\partial x = \Delta p_0\cos(kx - \omega t)$, $\Delta p_0 = Bks_0$: pressure is $90°$ out of phase with displacement, pressure nodes sit at displacement antinodes. Interactive: `owt-wave-lab` (mode `standing`, `boundary: "open-open"`, `harmonic: 1`) preview of air-layer dots. Audible range 20 Hz–20 kHz, infrasound, ultrasound. Worked examples: pressure amplitude for $s_0 = 10^{-6}$ m at 1 kHz in air ($B = 1.4\times10^5$ Pa, $v = 340$ m/s). Misconception killed: "maximum pressure is where the air moves most" |
| 2.2 | `speed-of-sound` | How Fast Sound Travels | $v = \sqrt{B/\rho}$; for a gas, Laplace's adiabatic $B = \gamma p$ gives $v = \sqrt{\gamma RT/M}$ | Derive by analogy with $\sqrt{T/\mu}$ (restoring stiffness over inertia). Interactive: `function-machine` (`expr: "331*sqrt((273 + x)/273)"`, `exprLatex: "v = 331\\sqrt{\\frac{273 + t}{273}}"`, input −20 to 60 °C, `inputUnit: "°C"`). | 2.3 | `intensity-and-loudness` | Intensity and the Decibel Scale | $I = \frac{\Delta p_0^2}{2\rho v} = 2\pi^2\rho v f^2 s_0^2$; $\beta = 10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$ | Derive $I$ from energy per volume × speed. | 2.4 | `organ-pipes-and-resonance` | Organ Pipes and the Resonance Tube | Open end = displacement antinode, closed end = node; open pipe $f_n = nv/2L$, closed pipe odd harmonics $(2n-1)v/4L$ | Interactive: `owt-wave-lab` (mode `standing`, `boundary: "closed-open"`, `waveSpeed: 340`, `stringLength: 0.5`, `sliders: ["harmonic","stringLength"]`) then `boundary: "open-open"`; count N and A. | 2.5 | `beats` | Beats | Two close frequencies drift in and out of step: loudness pulses $\lvert f_1 - f_2\rvert$ times a second | Derive $y = 2A\cos(\pi\Delta f\,t)\sin(\pi(f_1 + f_2)t)$ from the sum-to-product identity (trigonometry link). | 2.6 | `doppler-effect` | The Doppler Effect | Source motion squashes wavelengths; observer motion changes the rate crests are met: $f' = f\frac{v + v_o}{v - v_s}$ (towards) | Interactive: `owt-wave-lab` (mode `doppler`, `waveSpeed: 340`, `sourceFrequency: 500`, `sliders: ["sourceSpeed","observerSpeed"]`), Play to watch crests bunch ahead. ## Chapter 4: Heat and Kinetic Theory

**Slug:** `heat-and-kinetic-theory`

**Purpose:** give temperature a meaning (molecular kinetic energy), follow heat as it expands things, changes phases and flows (conduction, convection, radiation), and derive gas pressure and heat capacities from molecules in motion.

| Lesson | Slug | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ---- | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 4.1 | `temperature-and-thermometry` | Temperature and Thermometers | Zeroth law makes temperature measurable; any linear property defines a scale | Hook: a metal and a wooden bench at the same temperature feel different. Zeroth law. Two-fixed-point calibration $\frac{t - 0}{100} = \frac{X - X_0}{X_{100} - X_0}$ derived for any thermometric property; C, F, K conversion (−40 coincidence). Constant-volume gas thermometer and absolute zero by extrapolation. Interactive: `function-machine` (`expr: "1.8*x + 32"`, `exprLatex: "F = \\tfrac95 C + 32"`, input −50 to 110 °C, `inputUnit: "°C"`, `outputLabel: "Fahrenheit"`). Worked examples: faulty thermometer reading 5 at ice point and 95 at steam point; resistance thermometer. Misconception killed: "heat and temperature are the same thing" |
| 4.2 | `thermal-expansion` | Thermal Expansion | $\Delta L = L\alpha\Delta T$; $\beta \approx 2\alpha$, $\gamma \approx 3\alpha$ | Molecular reason (asymmetric PE well). Area and volume coefficients derived by expanding $(1 + \alpha\Delta T)^2$ and $(\ldots)^3$. Applications: railway gaps, bimetallic strip, holes expand like the material, pendulum clock error $\Delta T/T = \tfrac12\alpha\Delta\theta$ (link 0.5), real vs apparent expansion of liquids, anomalous expansion of water (lakes freeze top-down), thermal stress $Y\alpha\Delta T$ (link 3.2). | 4.3 | `calorimetry-and-latent-heat` | Calorimetry and Change of State | Heat lost = heat gained; $Q = mc\Delta T$ changes temperature, $Q = mL$ changes phase at constant temperature | Specific and molar heat capacity, water equivalent. Heating curve of ice → steam (plateaus). Method callout: first check whether all the ice melts. Interactive: `graph-explorer` or a table of the heating curve. Worked examples: 100 g ice at −10 °C in 200 g water at 50 °C (final state); steam at 100 °C into water; bullet melting on impact (energy link). Misconception killed: "adding heat always raises temperature" |
| 4.4 | `conduction` | Conduction | $\frac{dQ}{dt} = \frac{kA\,\Delta T}{L}$; thermal resistance $R = L/kA$ combines like electrical resistance | Fourier's law, temperature gradient, steady state. Worked examples: two slabs in series, find interface temperature; rods in parallel; time for ice to thicken from 1 cm to 2 cm vs 2 to 3 cm. Misconception killed: "a better conductor always feels hotter" (it feels hotter or colder, whichever way heat flows faster) |
| 4.5 | `convection-and-radiation` | Convection, Radiation and Cooling | Radiated power $P = e\sigma AT^4$; Wien $\lambda_m T = b$; small differences cool exponentially (Newton) | Convection qualitatively (sea breeze). Black body, emissivity, absorptivity, Kirchhoff's law (good absorber = good emitter). Stefan–Boltzmann net loss $e\sigma A(T^4 - T_0^4)$. Wien's law and star colours. Newton's law of cooling derived from Stefan for small $\Delta T$; exponential decay and the averaged-temperature JEE shortcut. Interactive: `graph-explorer` (`expr: "20 + 60*exp(-0.1*x)"`, window 0–20 × 0–90) cooling curve. Worked examples: sphere temperature doubled → power ×16; sun's surface temperature from $\lambda_m$; body cools 80 → 60 °C in 5 min, time for 60 → 40 °C. Misconception killed: "a body at room temperature does not radiate" |
| 4.6 | `kinetic-theory-of-gases` | Kinetic Theory of Gases | Molecular collisions give $p = \tfrac13\rho v_{\text{rms}}^2$, so $\tfrac12mv_{\text{rms}}^2 = \tfrac32k_BT$ | Assumptions of an ideal gas. Derive pressure from momentum change per collision in a cube. Temperature as mean KE; $v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{3RT/M}$, $v_{\text{avg}}$, $v_{\text{mp}}$ and their ratio. Interactive: `owt-thermo-lab` (mode `kinetic`, `sliders: ["temperature","volume"]`), heat → faster molecules, squeeze → same speeds, more wall hits. Mean free path $\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt2\pi d^2 n}$ (formula, qualitative). Ideal gas law and Dalton's law recovered. Worked examples: $v_{\text{rms}}$ of O₂ at 300 K; temperature at which H₂ has the $v_{\text{rms}}$ of O₂ at 27 °C; pressure change when speeds double. Misconceptions killed: "doubling temperature doubles $v_{\text{rms}}$" and "all molecules in a gas move at the same speed" |
| 4.7 | `equipartition-and-heat-capacities` | Degrees of Freedom and Equipartition | Each quadratic degree of freedom holds $\tfrac12k_BT$; $U = \frac f2 nRT$, $C_V = \frac f2 R$, $C_p = C_V + R$, $\gamma = 1 + \frac 2f$ | Count $f$: monatomic 3, rigid diatomic 5, with vibration 7, polyatomic 6. Mayer's relation $C_p - C_V = R$ derived (link 5.1). Table of $\gamma$. Mixtures: $C_V$ mole-weighted, $\gamma_{\text{mix}}$. Specific heat of solids $3R$ (Dulong–Petit). Misconception killed: "all ideal gases store the same energy at the same temperature" |
| 4.8 | `chapter-4-mastery` | Chapter 4 Mastery | Heat, expansion, flow and molecules together | Info callout "Chapter 4 in 8 lines". Mastery quizzes (10–12): scale conversion, faulty thermometer, expansion of a hole, pendulum clock error, mixing ice and water, slabs in series, Stefan $T^4$ scaling, Wien's law, Newton cooling times, $v_{\text{rms}}$ ratios, $\gamma$ of a mixture, JEE Advanced: rod clamped between walls heated (thermal stress + energy), sphere cooling by radiation with changing radius |

## Chapter 5: Thermodynamics

**Slug:** `thermodynamics`

**Purpose:** book-keeping for energy. Work is area under a p–V curve, the first law ties Q, W and ΔU, standard processes become shapes on a diagram, cycles turn heat into work, and the second law caps the efficiency at Carnot's.

| Lesson | Slug | Topic | Core idea | Planned content |
| ------ | ---- | ----- | --------- | --------------- |
| 5.1 | `first-law-of-thermodynamics` | Heat, Work and the First Law | $W = \int p\,dV$ is the area under the p–V curve; $\Delta U = Q - W$; $U$ depends only on state | System, surroundings, state variables vs path quantities. Derive $W = p\,A\,dx = p\,dV$ for a piston. Interactive: `owt-thermo-lab` (mode `process`, `process: "isobaric"`, `processToggle: true`), shaded area = W, compression area turns orange. Sign conventions (NCERT/JEE: W by the gas) with a warning callout for chemistry's opposite sign. $U$ is a state function: different paths, same ΔU, different Q and W. Worked examples: gas expands at 100 kPa from 10 L to 25 L (1500 J); two paths between the same states; heating water to steam at atmospheric pressure (ΔU from Q and W). Misconception killed: "heat is contained in a body" (a body has internal energy; heat is energy in transit) |
| 5.2 | `thermodynamic-processes` | Isochoric, Isobaric and Isothermal | Hold V, p or T fixed and read W, ΔU, Q from the first law | Interactive: `owt-thermo-lab` (mode `process`, `processToggle: true`, `showIsotherms: true`) stepping through isochoric ($W = 0$, $Q = nC_V\Delta T$), isobaric ($W = p\Delta V$, $Q = nC_p\Delta T$), isothermal ($\Delta U = 0$, $W = nRT\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1}$, derived by integration). Why $C_p > C_V$ (the extra heat does work), Mayer's relation. Worked examples: 2 mol monatomic gas heated at constant pressure by 50 K (Q, W, ΔU); isothermal doubling of volume at 300 K (1729 J per mol); ratio $Q_p/Q_V = \gamma$. Misconception killed: "no temperature change means no heat flows" (in an isothermal expansion all the absorbed heat becomes work) |
| 5.3 | `adiabatic-processes` | Adiabatic and Polytropic Processes | $Q = 0$ gives $pV^\gamma = $ const, $TV^{\gamma-1} = $ const, $W = \frac{p_1V_1 - p_2V_2}{\gamma - 1}$ | Derive $pV^\gamma$ from $nC_VdT = -p\,dV$ and $pV = nRT$. Interactive: `owt-thermo-lab` (mode `compare`, gas toggle): adiabat steeper than isotherm by γ, less work on expansion, gas cools. Examples: bicycle pump warming, cloud formation, diesel ignition. Worked examples: monatomic gas compressed adiabatically to 1/8 volume (T ×4, p ×32); work in adiabatic expansion; adiabatic vs isothermal final pressure. Misconception killed: "adiabatic means the temperature is constant" |
| 5.4 | `cyclic-processes-and-pv-diagrams` | Cycles and Reading Graphs | In a cycle ΔU = 0, so net Q = net W = enclosed area (clockwise positive) | Interactive: `owt-thermo-lab` (mode `cycle`, `cycle: "rectangle"`, `sliders: ["finalVolume","finalPressure"]`) with the per-leg Q, W, ΔU table. Direction: clockwise = engine, anticlockwise = refrigerator. Converting processes between p–V, V–T and p–T graphs (isochore is a straight line through the origin on p–T, etc.), a favourite JEE skill. Worked examples: triangular cycle area; which legs absorb heat in a given rectangle; convert a V–T cycle into a p–V diagram. Misconception killed: "net work is zero because the gas returns to its starting state" |
| 5.5 | `heat-engines-and-refrigerators` | Heat Engines and Refrigerators | $\eta = W/Q_H = 1 - Q_C/Q_H$; refrigerator COP $= Q_C/W$ | Reservoir diagram. Otto cycle (petrol engine) with $\eta = 1 - r^{1-\gamma}$ derived from the two adiabats: `owt-thermo-lab` (mode `cycle`, `cycle: "otto"`, `sliders: ["compressionRatio","pressureRatio"]`), efficiency depends on $r$ only. Refrigerator and heat pump, COP relation $\text{COP}_{\text{HP}} = 1 + \text{COP}_{\text{R}}$. Worked examples: engine absorbs 1000 J, rejects 600 J; Otto with $r = 8$, $\gamma = 1.4$ ($\approx 0.56$); refrigerator removing 300 J per cycle with 100 J of work. Misconception killed: "a better engine design could reach 100% efficiency" (forward to 5.6) |
| 5.6 | `second-law-and-carnot` | The Second Law and the Carnot Engine | No engine can convert heat entirely to work; the reversible Carnot engine sets the ceiling $\eta = 1 - T_C/T_H$ | Kelvin–Planck and Clausius statements and their equivalence (sketch). Reversible vs irreversible processes (friction, free expansion, finite ΔT). Carnot cycle built from two isotherms and two adiabats; derive $Q_C/Q_H = T_C/T_H$ using $V_C/V_D = V_B/V_A$. Interactive: `owt-thermo-lab` (mode `cycle`, `cycle: "carnot"`, `sliders: ["finalVolume","coldTemperature"]`), η readout matches $1 - T_C/T_H$ whatever the volumes or gas. Carnot's theorem. Entropy as $dQ_{\text{rev}}/T$ (brief, JEE Advanced flavour). Worked examples: engine between 500 K and 300 K (max 40%); claimed engine violating Carnot; lowering $T_C$ vs raising $T_H$ by the same amount (lowering $T_C$ wins). Misconception killed: "efficiency depends on the working gas" |
| 5.7 | `chapter-5-mastery` | Chapter 5 Mastery | Full energy bookkeeping, from scratch | Info callout "Chapter 5 in 8 lines". Mastery quizzes (10–12): work from a p–V graph, first law with signs, isobaric Q with $C_p$, isothermal work, adiabatic T and p ratios, isotherm vs adiabat slopes, polytropic heat capacity, net work in a cycle, V–T to p–V conversion, Otto and Carnot efficiencies, refrigerator COP, JEE Advanced: two-chamber piston problem (adiabatic partition) or a cycle with an isothermal and adiabatic leg where heat absorbed must be found leg by leg |
